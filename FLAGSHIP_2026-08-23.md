## Open note: Blue may be undervalued, and the instruments cannot see it

Both the dashboard mirror (45.8%) and the counter-matrix (54.8%) rank Blue mid-to-low. **Marc
believes Blue paired with freeze is one of the strongest builds in the game, and that it
requires cycling the freeze cooldown well to express.**

That is consistent rather than contradictory: every instrument here measures a team's value
*in HeuristicBot's hands*, and HeuristicBot fires gadgets on cooldown without sequencing them
(see the capability-gap note in the memory index). A build whose value comes from cooldown
cycling is therefore invisible to all of them — the measurement is of the bot's capability,
not the team's ceiling.

**Consequence: do not use the balance tables to justify a Blue or freeze nerf.** The honest
reading is "HeuristicBot cannot use Blue/freeze", not "Blue/freeze is weak". Testing this
needs either a bot that sequences cooldowns or Marc's own recorded games — his 4W/9L on Blue
is against the hardest counter in the matrix, so it is not evidence against him either.

## The repair fixes built on top of it (2026-08-23)

Three settings, **all defaulting to flagship behaviour**. With them off,
`bot-checksum --games 24` still prints `643A6CA19C1851CF04A2A0C9F873195C` — verified.

| setting | default | effect |
|---|---|---|
| `RepairPriceCheck` | `false` | applies `RepairBuysItsPrice` to the shipped bot (today `!DefenceOnly` makes it dead code there) |
| `RepairHpFloorPct` | `0` | repair below this fraction of max HP regardless of time-to-death |
| `RepairMinIntervalSeconds` | `0` | minimum gap between repairs |

`HeuristicBotSettings.RepairFixProfile` = price check on, floor **45%**, interval **1.0s**.
Floor chosen from the two under-repair games, which sat at 41% and 19% of maximum for ten
seconds or more; interval chosen so a repair lands and the threat is re-read before another
can fire.

Enable for play with `"Singleplayer": { "RepairFix": true }`. Games are recorded as
`opponent_type = "heuristic_repairfix"` so the two arms are separable in `game_records.db`.

**Measured bot-vs-bot, repair-fix in seat 1 against the flagship in seat 2, n=200 paired:**

- **win rate 50.5%** — indistinguishable from 50% (SE 3.5pp). No measurable gain, no harm.
- **behaviour genuinely changes in 192 of 200 games**: 3.96 repairs/game against the
  flagship's 4.58 (−14%), but **more** repairs in 87 games and **fewer** in 105.

That two-sided split is the point and is the evidence the change is real rather than dead
code: the HP floor adds repairs in the games where the flagship under-repaired, the price
check and interval remove them where it panicked. A blanket reduction would have meant only
one of the three knobs was doing anything.

**Bot-vs-bot cannot settle this.** Both failures were identified in games against a human, in
situations a human creates — a Wave pushback leaving a one-hit window, or a threatened spike
that triggers a seven-repair panic. Neither arises reliably in self-play, which is why the
head-to-head is flat. The test is Marc playing it.

## The repair fixes built on top of it (2026-08-23)

Three settings, **all defaulting to flagship behaviour**. With them off,
`bot-checksum --games 24` still prints `643A6CA19C1851CF04A2A0C9F873195C` — verified.

| setting | default | effect |
|---|---|---|
| `RepairPriceCheck` | `false` | applies `RepairBuysItsPrice` to the shipped bot (today `!DefenceOnly` makes it dead code there) |
| `RepairHpFloorPct` | `0` | repair below this fraction of max HP regardless of time-to-death |
| `RepairMinIntervalSeconds` | `0` | minimum gap between repairs |

`HeuristicBotSettings.RepairFixProfile` = price check on, floor **45%**, interval **1.0s**.
Floor chosen from the two under-repair games, which sat at 41% and 19% of maximum for ten
seconds or more; interval chosen so a repair lands and the threat is re-read before another
can fire.

Enable for play with `"Singleplayer": { "RepairFix": true }`. Games are recorded as
`opponent_type = "heuristic_repairfix"` so the two arms are separable in `game_records.db`.

**Measured bot-vs-bot, repair-fix in seat 1 against the flagship in seat 2, n=200 paired:**

- **win rate 50.5%** — indistinguishable from 50% (SE 3.5pp). No measurable gain, no harm.
- **behaviour genuinely changes in 192 of 200 games**: 3.96 repairs/game against the
  flagship's 4.58 (−14%), but **more** repairs in 87 games and **fewer** in 105.

That two-sided split is the point and is the evidence the change is real rather than dead
code: the HP floor adds repairs in the games where the flagship under-repaired, the price
check and interval remove them where it panicked. A blanket reduction would have meant only
one of the three knobs was doing anything.

**Bot-vs-bot cannot settle this.** Both failures were identified in games against a human, in
situations a human creates — a Wave pushback leaving a one-hit window, or a threatened spike
that triggers a seven-repair panic. Neither arises reliably in self-play, which is why the
head-to-head is flat. The test is Marc playing it.

## THE FINGERPRINT MOVED, AND IT WAS THE GAME DATA, NOT THE BOT

`bot-checksum --games 24` now prints **`33DD370A7EA7E402F7B2DBBEDA76BFC8`**, not
`643A6CA19C1851CF04A2A0C9F873195C`.

**Cause: `master_gadgets.csv` was edited on 2026-08-24 at 12:08** — `divine_3` BaseValue
`0` → `10000`, i.e. the Divine Shield signature went from granting no shield to granting
10,000. Yellow's signature gadget changing changes how every game involving Yellow resolves,
so the behavioural hash moves. That file is uncommitted, so the flagship tag does **not**
pin it.

Established by bisection, not by assumption: reverting the repair gate and the hazard gate
independently still produced `33DD…`, and the restored code with all flags off produces
`33DD…` as well — identical to the untouched bot on the same data.

**Two consequences.**

1. **The flag-off invariant still holds.** Every setting added after the freeze is
   behaviour-neutral when off; the bot is byte-identical to stock on whatever data it is given.
   That is what the guard was for and it is intact.
2. **`643A…` is only reproducible with the pre-2026-08-24 gadget table.** Restoring the
   flagship needs the data as well as the code. Marc's 19–9 record was played on the old
   `divine_3`.

**The tag should be treated as code-only.** To make the restore point complete, either commit
`master_gadgets.csv` at the state the flagship was measured on, or re-baseline the flagship
fingerprint to `33DD…` and accept the new gadget table as part of it. That is Marc's call —
the record here is that the two are currently out of step.

Going forward the guard is **`33DD370A7EA7E402F7B2DBBEDA76BFC8`**, valid only while the gadget
table is unchanged. A fingerprint mismatch means "something changed", not "the bot changed" —
check `git status` on `CastleDefense.Engine/Data/` before assuming it was code.

## The hazard attack blackout (2026-08-24)

`HazardAttackBlackout`, default `false`. Stops buying **attacking** units while an enemy-cast
Wave or Blackhole hazard is live, so the money goes on the ladder instead.

Marc: *"the Blue team's Wave gadget makes it effectively immune to unit damage while it's
active… the bot keeps up its attack in this scenario, effectively wasting money trying to apply
pressure when there is physically no way to do so."*

The engine agrees. `WaveHazard.ProcessEffect` knocks every non-wall enemy unit inside its span
back by up to 3,000px at level 3 as it sweeps; `BlackholeHazard` drags units to its centre for
its full duration. Blue's signature is `wave`, Black's is `blackhole`.

**Attack-only by design.** Reactive defence keeps buying — a pushed-around blocker is still a
body the attacker has to chew through, and gating defence on an enemy gadget would hand the
opponent a switch to turn the bot's defence off.

Measured, repair+hazard in seat 1 against the flagship in seat 2, n=300 paired:

- **49.3% win rate** — indistinguishable from 50% (SE 2.9pp).
- **Fires in 127/300 games**, median 181 decisions suppressed, max 692.

**Where it fires, checked rather than assumed.** It triggers in **100% of games** where the
opponent is Blue or Black — it never misses one. The remaining 35 games have a non-Blue,
non-Black opponent, which looked like a scoping bug and is not: **`ArmageddonEffect` casts
`wave_3`** regardless of team, so those are the gate correctly refusing to attack into an
Armageddon tsunami. Harmless there — that game is already decided — but it is why the raw
"42% of games" figure is not the same as "25% of opponents are Blue or Black".

Playable via `"Singleplayer": { "RepairFix": true, "HazardFix": true }`. Games record as
`opponent_type = "heuristic_repair_hazard"`.

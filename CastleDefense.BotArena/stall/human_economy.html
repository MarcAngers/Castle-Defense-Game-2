The near-death at 94s is the strategy, not a mistake

Marc bottoms out at 999 HP while holding money he could have spent on defence. He does not spend it. He is buying rungs instead, and taking the damage is what pays for them — the castle is a resource he is willing to spend down to almost nothing.

The defence-only bot never does this. Its whole option comparison prices a purchase against dying, so as its survival estimate falls it spends harder, which is the opposite response.

He is BEHIND for six rungs and then wins the two that matter

Through rung 6 Marc is consistently a few seconds slower than his opponent (121s against 115s), and slower than the defence-only bot too. Nothing in the first two minutes looks like winning.

Then he takes rung 7 at 153s while the bot does not reach it until 221s, and rung 8 at 226s while the bot never does. The defence-only bot's mirror is the exact inverse: it reaches rung 7 first (147s to 151s) and then its opponent takes rung 8 at 227s and it never does.

04

What this changes

Marc's total unit spend here is $24,569, against the defence-only bot's $85,229 in its mirror. He is not spending less overall — he is spending it later. Every dollar before 152s goes into the ladder, and the army only appears once the income is there to pay for it.

So the defence-only premise — defend cheaply, out-invest, win late — is vindicated, and the bot is failing at the part it was built for. It spends $85,229 defending and finishes a rung behind. Marc spends nothing for 152 seconds and finishes a rung ahead.

The gap is not which unit to buy or when to wipe. It is that Marc treats castle health as something to spend, and the bot treats it as something to protect.

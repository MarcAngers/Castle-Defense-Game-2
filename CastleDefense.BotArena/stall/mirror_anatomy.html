01

Castle health, both builds

Two charts rather than one with two vertical scales: health peaks at 78,000 and incoming damage at 29,990, so a shared axis would flatten one and invent a crossing point that means nothing.

The shape changed, not just the numbers

The old line is a cliff — a tall repair-funded spike to 78,000 and then 24,045 health gone in a single second. The new line never gets that high and never falls that fast: its worst moment is three times shallower, and it grinds down through three similar windows instead of being deleted in one.

Peak incoming damage also dropped by a third, from 29,990 to 19,750. That is the wipes landing — the enemy army is being cut down rather than accumulating unopposed.

03

The economy, both players

Money on hand through the current build's game. The ticks along the bottom mark investments — the sawtooth is the economy laddering up, not spending.

Identical economies — which kills the premise

Both players finish on income $750/s having earned 7 investments each. The defence-only bot is not out-earning anybody. The whole case for playing this way was "defend cheaply, invest faster, reach Armageddon first" — and the ladders are the same height.

What differs is what the money became. We spent $18,581 on units and they spent $8,636; they finish holding $38,112 against our $22,592. They are richer at the end because we never threaten them — nothing forces them to convert cash into defence, so their spending is discretionary and ours is not.

Our line sits above $5,000 for 34% of the game, up from 16% before the repair fix. Some of that is healthy — it is money not being burned on $8,837 repairs — but it is also money the bot never finds a use for while it is being killed.

Investment 7 is where it stops choosing

Through investments 3 to 5 the bot is in watch almost the whole time — correctly, nothing is threatening it. At investment 6 it fights its first real engagement and spends most of it holding, which is the branch working as designed: it lets the wave form and answers it.

Then at investment 7 it spends 68% of the level in critical. That is not a decision, it is the absence of one — the survival law has it permanently inside its own death window, so the dollar comparison never runs and it just blocks at maximum rate. wipe fires 2% of the time, at exactly the point in the game where a single unit could clear the biggest army it will ever face.

Across the whole game: 56% idle, 15% holding, 3% spending, 26% critical — so only 3% of the game is spent actually buying anything, and more than a quarter of it is spent past the point where the bot still has a choice.

01

Castle health, both builds

Two charts rather than one with two vertical scales: health peaks at 78,000 and incoming damage at 171,630, so a shared axis would flatten one and invent a crossing point that means nothing.

The shape changed, not just the numbers

The old line is a cliff — a repair-funded spike to 78,000 and then 24,045 health gone per second. The current line never gets that high and never falls that fast: its worst moment is 3.5 times shallower (6,930 HP/s), and it grinds down through several similar windows instead of being deleted in one.

Peak incoming damage went the other way — 29,990 to 171,630, 5.7 times worse. An earlier draft of this page read that as the wipes landing, back when the number fell. It rose because the bot now lives 93 seconds longer, and the opponent's economy compounds through every one of them. Surviving longer means meeting a bigger army, so peak DPS is a clock reading, not a scoreboard — the honest measure is the slope of our health, and that is 3.5 times shallower.

03

The economy, both players

Money on hand through the current build's game. The ticks along the bottom mark investments — the sawtooth is the economy laddering up, not spending.

It wins the economic race for 7 rungs and loses it on the eighth

The two ladders are lock-step identical through investment 7 — the same 7 rungs at the same seconds, and we reach the last shared one 3 seconds ahead of them (148s against 151s). The premise of the whole defence-only design — defend cheaply, keep pace, reach Armageddon — is working exactly as intended, right up to the rung that decides the game.

Then they buy investment 8 at 221s, their income triples to $2,500/s, and we never buy it at all. We die 65 seconds later.

The eighth investment costs $40,000. In the 138 seconds we had, we earned $103,425 and spent $76,935 of it on defence — against the $33,088 they spent attacking. Our cash peaked at $21,650: 54% of the price.

Correction. An earlier version of this page called investment 8 "Armageddon, and a guaranteed win". It is not. `GameEngine.Invest` fires Armageddon when the count is already 8 — the ninth purchase — and leaves income alone, so investment 8 is an ordinary economy rung and Armageddon costs $121,221 on top of it. That makes the gap far wider than the paragraph above implies: the bot was not one affordable rung from winning, it was two, and the second is 3x the price of the first.

Our line sits above $5,000 for 50% of the game, up from 16% before the repair fix. That is money not being burned on $8,837 repairs — but the defensive comparison prices a blocker against dying and never against the $40,000 that ends the game, so a rung it could reach stays invisible to it.

Investment 7 is where the game is decided — and it is choosing again

Through investments 3 to 5 the bot is in watch almost the whole time — correctly, nothing is threatening it. At investment 6 it fights its first real engagement and spends most of it holding, which is the branch working as designed: it lets the wave form and answers it.

Investment 7 is then 138 seconds long, half the game, and the whole outcome sits inside it. The previous build spent 68% of that level in critical — not a decision but the absence of one, the survival law holding it permanently inside its own death window so the dollar comparison never ran. It is now 39%, with 20% block, 14% outmatched and 6% wipe — wipes firing 3 times as often as before, at exactly the point where one unit can clear the biggest army it will ever face.

That is the intended machinery finally running under load. It is also still a loss, which is the point worth keeping: the comparison now works and picks the best available option, and the best available option is not good enough.

Across the whole game: 46% idle, 16% holding, 13% spending, 24% critical — so only 13% of the game is spent actually buying anything, and more than a quarter of it is spent past the point where the bot still has a choice.
